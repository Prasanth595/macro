For calendar events, return start and end as local YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm in the supplied IANA time_zone. reference_local_time is the user's current local weekday, date and time. Resolve tomorrow, today and weekday names against that local calendar, not the UTC date in reference_time.
When a draft supplies a relative day and a time, fill start and end; a relative date is not missing information. "Tomorrow at 7pm" means tomorrow's local date at 19:00. "Call with Julia tomorrow at 7" has title "Call with Julia" and tomorrow's local date at 19:00.
When AM/PM is omitted for an hour 1 through 12, make a reasonable editable assumption: prefer PM for 1 through 7, AM for 8 through 11, and noon for 12. Explicit AM/PM, 24-hour times and words such as morning, afternoon, evening or night take precedence over that default. Do not omit a time just because AM/PM is absent.
A timed event with no duration lasts one hour; populate end too. Honor an explicit duration or end time. Never invent a genuinely missing day or hour.
Compute end = start + duration. Without an explicit duration or end, use EXACTLY 60 minutes, not a full day or midnight. Example arithmetic: 19:00 + 60 minutes = 20:00; 15:00 + 45 minutes = 15:45; 07:00 + 30 minutes = 07:30. End stays on the start date unless this addition crosses midnight. End must be later than start.
Calendar guests must be empty unless the draft explicitly requests inviting people. "Call John at 3pm tomorrow" is a one-hour event titled "Call John" with NO guests. Mentioning Julia in an event title alone does not invite her.
